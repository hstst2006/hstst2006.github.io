# Motivation
I have wanted to make a game for a long time, and the plan is to make a typing game. Even though this is a niche genre, there are tons of simple "typing tutor" games out there, and even a few "bigger" games with more mainstream appeal. <!--The gameplay mechanics of these games vary somewhat, but stays relatively consistent at their core. These simple gameplay mechanics makes the genre well suited for short playsessions, player made wordlists, endless mode, and scoreboards based on accuracy and speed. -->

# Examples of typing games for inspiration
## The simple kinds
The simples kinds of typing games are exactly as the name of the genre would suggest; games where you type. You can easily find the simpler versions of typing games free online. These are games where you either test your typing speed and accuracy by typing a text. 

 "Typing tutors" are games where the words scroll across the screen and you attempt to complete them before they scroll past you. A well made example of the latter is [Ztype](https://zty.pe/), where you controll a small spaceship and destroy an increasing number of enemies in waves.

## The "advanced" kinds
These games are not necessarily more advanced in terms of gameplay mechanics, the typing tutor style is usually a core gameplay components. These games provide an experience most people would consider more "game-like" in terms of storytelling, gameplay variation and graphics. Examples of such games are for example: Nanotale, Epistory and Typing of the Dead: Overkill.

Nanotale and Epistory are made by the same developers, and thus share many similar game mechanics. In these games your character is able to move, and gathers different powers through the game that enhances your character and allows you to solve environmental puzzles. For the most part you are able to choose whenever you want to move, or start typing to attack enemies, or interact with the world around you.

Typing of the Dead: Overkill is an on rails experience, and is much closer to a traditional "typing tutor" experience than Nanotale and Epistory is. Your character moves on its own through the levels and fight zombies and bosses by typing. What sets Typing of the Dead apart from simpler games like Ztype is (crudely put) the graphics and storytelling that makes for a more complete gaming experience.

# The gameplay idea
Playing on the idea of a story driven typing game from the titles mentioned above: I want a game where the player is:
* Able to move freely across the map
* Fights enemies using typing tutor mechanics
* Fights bosses using typing mechanics (Longer texts)
* Progresses through a simple story.
* Progresses through a small sample of differently themed(?) maps.

Nice to haves:
* Puzzles
* Collectibles
* Option to add custom content
    * Custom word lists/languages
    * Endless mode with custom word lists
    * Custom enemies
* Options to change fonts and font size
